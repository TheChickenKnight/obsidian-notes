# Agricultural Revolution
**circa 1000-1300 C.E.**
Warmer climate, expanding farmland, and new technology transformed medieval agriculture.
## Medieval Climate Anomaly
*aka Medieval Warm Period*
- A period of slightly warmer weather across the North-Atlantic region.
- Was not Global Warming! Solely local.
- Longer summers, fewer frosts, and steady rainfall produced more food, predictably.
- This environmental stability allowed farmers to not just expand but test, take risks in order for further development.
## Three-Field System
New revolution, as apposed to the old two-field.
- Land was divided into three fields instead of two:
	- Winter crop
		- wheat, etc.
	- Spring crop
		- oats barely or beans.
	- Fallow
		- grazed by animals, not for growing
		- These animals, obviously produce manure
			- used in fertilizing crop's soil
			- also used in brick making
- Each year, fields rotated
- This made it so that two thirds of land produced crops instead of one half.
- Reduces soil exhaustion
- Reduces pests.
## Technological Innovations
New tolls and sources of power improve productivity
### Water Mills and Windmills
Thousands of watermills by **1000 C.E.**, windmills spread from **circa 1200 C.E.**
- Harnessed natural power for:
	- grinding grain
	- sawing
	- fulling cloth
	- metalwork
- Freed humans and animals for other tasks.
- Mills were often owned by nobles and monasteries, and used by everyone
### Heavy Wheeled Plow
**aka Moldboard Plow**
- Cuts deep into the dense northern soils.
- Plows turn to aerate the earth and bring nutrients to the surface
- Allowed for richer earth
- Requires more power, encouraging teamwork and animal taming.
	- The previous best plow was called the "scratch plow"; It was much lighter.
### Upgrades to Horseshoes and Horse collars
- Early throat-girth harness would choke animals under heavy strain
- Added padded shoulder for distributing weight
- Horses are faster and easier to work with than oxen.
- Nailed Iron horseshoes protects hoof from rocky soil.
	- Before, temporary covers were used called "Hippo sandals"
# Medieval Rural Communities
## Village
community of peasants. A cluster of homes sharing open fields and resources
## Manor
Lord's estate - rents, courts, obligations
### Demesne
Land that is worked by peasants, solely for the Lord
## Parish
Organized spiritual life, led by church.priest
### Tithes
mandatory tax paid to parish
### Glebe
Land that feeds the priest.
# Social Hierarchy
## Slaves
Unfree persons owned outright (gradually disappeared by **1100s C.E.**)
## Serfs
Bound to the land, owes labor and dues to the lord.
## Free peasants
pays rent for land, maybe military. Had greater mobility.
## Reeve
Manages peasants/labor/collected rents for the lord.
# The Medieval Bakehouse
- Shared bakehouse $\rightarrow$ peasants paid fees to lords.

# Pannage
A fee paid by peasants so their pigs could eat acorns in the lord's forest.
As the lord owns all of the forest, so anything done in must be paid to the lord.
# Peasant Dwellings
**Wattle and Daub Houses**
- Peasant houses were made with wattle (woven wood) and daub (mud, straw, dung)
- Thatched roofs, often no chimneys.
- Floors of packed earth
- Single multi-purpose room
- Materials all locally sourced and communal.
